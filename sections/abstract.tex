Glioblastoma (GBM) prognosis remains difficult because bulk omics obscure cell-state heterogeneity and strict cross-cohort generalization is rarely tested. We constructed a cross-scale GBM benchmark that combines CPTAC-3 bulk RNA-seq and methylation data with six single-cell RNA-seq-derived marker programs transferred from 17 GBM cases. The strict external benchmark trains on CPTAC (195 RNA+clinical cases) and tests on TCGA-GBM (286 cases), while a separate CPTAC ablation quantifies the value of methylation and scRNA-derived priors. We evaluated logistic, LASSO, ridge, SVM, random forest, gradient boosting, Self-Normalizing Network, compact transformer, and a prior-guided kernel transformer with biologically masked attention and learnable radial basis mapping. Under external validation, the compact transformer achieved the best 1-year AUC (0.581) and C-index (0.553), whereas LASSO logistic achieved the best 3-year AUC (0.612); the prior-guided kernel transformer reached 0.516 and 0.586 AUC at the 1-year and 3-year horizons, respectively. Internal ablation showed that methylation contributed the strongest gain, increasing the 3-year SVM AUC from 0.527 to 0.666, while the current scRNA program scores produced only marginal changes. These results define both the promise and the present limitation of prior-guided cross-scale fusion for GBM prognosis.
